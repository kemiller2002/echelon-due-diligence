# Commercial opportunity assessment

Every market statement below is a **hypothesis** unless a source ID is given. No market
sizing, pricing or competitor revenue data was gathered in this research phase; the
assessment says what would have to be true and how to test it cheaply.

## 1. Three distinct opportunities

| # | Opportunity | Who pays | What Echelon sells | Evidence today |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| O1 | **Internal: lower cost of being assessed** | Echelon (cost avoided) | Nothing; faster, cheaper, more credible responses for Unbound and other Echelon products | Strong rationale; savings unmeasured (`cost-avoidance/COMPLIANCE-COST-AVOIDANCE-REPORT.md` H1 to H12) |
| O2 | **DAF sponsor vendor-assessment capability** | DAF sponsors and community foundations evaluating technology vendors | A sponsor-side assessment kit and diagnostic engagement built from the DAF-specific question bank (DAF, GRT, CHV, CON, DON, WLB, PAY, RCN categories) and the sponsor audit model (`SRC-ECH-DAF-TECH-AUDIT`, C01 to C24) | No sponsor publishes vendor security requirements (DD-IND-DAF-001), suggesting an unmet need, or suggesting sponsors do not prioritize it. Untested |
| O3 | **Productized "due diligence as an output" for small regulated-market vendors** | Small fintech, wealth-tech and nonprofit-tech vendors | Evidence architecture, honesty-enforced answer library, AQR and generated trust center | Crowded market (trust center and questionnaire automation platforms exist: SRC-Q-CONVEYOR-DOC-ACCESS, SRC-Q-VANTA-TC-NDA, SRC-Q-DRATA-TRUST-CENTER); differentiation unproven |

## 2. Assessment

### O1: internal cost avoidance (pursue now)

- Unbound will face bank-style instruments (SIG, bank-built questionnaires) via
  financial-institution-affiliated sponsors (DD-SRC-SEC-001 conclusion 1). Each
  questionnaire is 200 to 1,000+ items; without a knowledge base each is a manual
  project. The bank now holds 2,319 canonical questions with honest status.
- The value is realized only if answers are true and evidence exists. Until then the
  knowledge base mainly tells Echelon what it cannot yet say, which is itself the most
  valuable near-term output (it orders the roadmap).
- Measure: hours per questionnaire, items answered from the bank without edit, reviewer
  edit rate, sales-cycle delay attributable to security review.

### O2: DAF sponsor assessment kit (test cheaply)

Why it might work:
- The DAF market has documented provider exits and assurance-scope confusion
  (`SRC-ECH-DAF-TECH-AUDIT`: incumbent marketing of SOC reports, provider shutdowns).
- An incumbent administrator publicly cites only a SOC 2 Type 1 (DD-IND-DAF-001), so the
  bar sponsors currently accept may be low, and a rigorous kit could raise it.
- The business plan already describes paid diagnostic engagements
  (`echelon-organization-administration@2763e576:business-plan/05-offer-architecture/05.02-paid-diagnostic-engagements.md`).

Why it might not:
- **Conflict of interest.** Echelon would assess vendors while selling Unbound against
  them. Sponsors and competitors may reject the kit as self-serving. Mitigation options:
  publish the kit openly (methodology as marketing, not revenue), or separate the entity
  and disclose. This conflict must be resolved before O2 is offered as paid work.
- Sponsors may not buy assessment help at all; small sponsors outsource to the
  administrator they would be assessing.
- Licensing: the kit cannot embed SIG, VSA or CAIQ text (DD-SRC-SEC-001 licensing
  constraints); it must use Echelon's own questions.

Cheapest test: publish the C01 to C24 sponsor audit questions with DAF-specific
canonical questions as a free guide; count sponsor downloads and conversations;
ask three sponsors whether they would pay for an assisted assessment.

### O3: productized platform (defer)

- Differentiators Echelon could credibly claim, once built and proven: the
  answer-status honesty rule enforced by tooling; evidence generated by construction
  and signed with open verification; architecture-aware penetration test scoping; F#
  security analyzers. None exists as a product today.
- The questionnaire automation and trust center categories are occupied by funded
  platforms; competing on generic features is unattractive for a small company.
- Credible wedge: open-source the honesty-enforcing record format, schemas and F#
  analyzers (if licensing changes from PolyForm Noncommercial to a permissive license,
  see OQ-DD-LIC-001), and earn credibility rather than license revenue.
- Defer until O1 has produced measured savings on at least five real questionnaires.

## 3. Strategic value independent of revenue

- **Sales friction:** a sponsor's vendor review is a gate, not a differentiator, until
  incumbents are weak on it. Passing quickly and honestly shortens cycles.
- **Credibility through candor:** the honest-status trust center (DD-TC-002) is unusual.
  Whether candor helps a pre-revenue vendor is an open question (OQ-DD-TC-002).
- **Shared assurance across products:** controls and evidence for Praxis-governed
  repositories, Tutela gates and Limen boundaries apply to every Echelon product, so
  marginal assurance cost per product should fall. Unmeasured.

## 4. Risks to the commercial thesis

- Echelon's organizational weaknesses (formation TODO, no insurance, no policies, key
  person dependence, noncommercial tool licenses) block sales before product strengths
  matter. These are cheap to fix relative to product work and are on the roadmap's P0
  list.
- Overclaiming security by construction would be more damaging than not claiming it
  (see adversarial reviews in `validation/`).
- AI-built software and AI-completed questionnaires invite skepticism; Echelon's answer
  must be evidence of review and gates, not assertion.

## 5. Recommendation

Pursue O1 immediately. Run the O2 low-cost test after resolving the conflict of
interest position. Defer O3, but keep the architecture product-ready (open formats,
open verification) so the option is not foreclosed.
